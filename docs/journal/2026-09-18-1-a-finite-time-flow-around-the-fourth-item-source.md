# 2026-09-18-1 — a finite-time flow around the fourth item source

Docs-only note. No code, no scope commitment.

Companion to kiss-ai's 2026-09-18 journal entry (`~/Development/work/kiss-ai/journal/2026-09-18.md`)
— that one holds the TCSearch side (a second documentation-building-search worked example:
ACL injury prevention, prompted by a friend's injury, same shape as the cello bow-strength
example from 2026-07-30 that's already recorded here under [the fourth item
source](../PROGRESS.md#-possible-fourth-item-source-compiled-skill-resource-documents-noted-2026-07-30)).
This one holds the part that's actually about this project.

Ryan named a flow, not just a search:

1. Notice something worth contemplating — a friend's ACL tear prompting "could that happen to
   me, and is there a way to prevent it."
2. Research it properly — TCSearch's compiled-document output, once it exists.
3. Decide whether and how to build it into daily life.
4. Set a plan toward it, including a long-term one — not just "do this today."
5. Weigh it against everything else already claiming time, **deliberately**, because time is
   finite. Two things bundled in his own framing: a new practice can *serve* existing goals at
   once (ACL-prevention work likely overlaps a strength/mobility goal already on the list, not
   a separate bucket), and taking it on means something else gets less time, on purpose rather
   than by drift.

Step 2 isn't this project's job — that's TCSearch, and it's still blocked on kiss-ai's own
chat-export migration, same as it's been since 07-02. Steps 1, 3, and 4 are already close to
what this app is for: the README's own pitch is "cycling old skills back in... pulling new
ones into regular rotation," and the balance board already does time-accounting *within*
what's on the list.

**Step 5 is the part not already here.** Checked before writing this — nothing in `README.md`
or `PROGRESS.md` names the decision of *adding* a time-option as a trade-off against something
specific. What exists today (category-balanced selection, the history rollups) answers "how
has my time actually split," after the fact, across whatever's already on the list. What Ryan
described is a decision *before* the fact: when cycling something new in, see what it would
cost — in category terms, in hours, against a stated goal — rather than adding it and letting
the balance-board's ongoing selection quietly absorb the change.

**Not designing this now.** No item-management verb, no model change, no UI. Recording it here
because it's a real gap between what's built and what Ryan described, and because it would be
awkward to notice only after the item-source interface (BV / custom / YouTube / the future
compiled-doc source) hardens further. Whenever that interface — or the add-item flow — gets
worked on next, this is worth having in view: not "does adding a time-option feel easy" but
"does adding one make its cost, and what it's meant to serve, visible at the moment of
deciding."

Status otherwise unchanged — Phase 2 (`interval` cadence, snooze, live-refreshing list, per
ADR-0005) is still next; this doesn't touch it.
